## 1. Starting position

**[HYP — proposed foundation]**

> **Reality consists in relational distinction. The reality under investigation is the complete relational whole within which its distinctions are constituted.**

"Relational distinction" is primitive at this stage. Its organization, admissible forms, and consequences remain to be investigated. "Distinction" here means relational distinction, not numerical difference or subtraction; no numerical representation (difference, ratio, phase, coordinate) is presupposed. Compare NUMGEN SG-NG-22.

## 2. Foundational core

### A1. Relational existence

**[HYP — proposed foundational commitment]**

> **Relational distinction is constitutive of existence within this framework. Existence is not identified with recognition: a distinction need not be registered by another part of the whole in order to belong to its relational organization.**

Registration and retention are further capacities to explain (Q6). This premise does not determine what participates in relations or how participants acquire identity.

*Note.* OFN v0.8 retains existence-as-recognisability as a hypothesis, not an adopted definition. A1 states the alternative choice explicitly for this exploratory opening; it records no change to the canonical documents.

### A2. Relational domain and closure

**[DEF — working domain definition]**

> **A relational domain is a whole specified through its participants, relations, and conditions of organization.** The terms "participant" and "relation" do not yet determine which is ontologically prior.

**[HYP — proposed closure commitment]**

> **The reality under investigation is relationally closed: every contribution required to determine its relational content must be included in its complete account. No unrepresented external contribution may be invoked to explain its organization or continuation.**

Closure is completeness of the relational account, not spatial enclosure. It does not establish that any presently written model is complete.

Closure requires that **any rule invoked to determine admissible configurations or their continuation enter the complete account explicitly**. It does not require such a rule to be an additional dynamical constituent. It also does not select among the open possibilities for continuation (Q5): derived from relational organization, constrained by an explicitly adopted principle, or recovered from an atemporal organization.

### A3. Structural equivalence (model-relative)

**[DEF — working equivalence criterion]**

> **Two relational descriptions are structurally equivalent relative to their declared structure when a correspondence preserves all declared relations, role types, multiplicities, constraints, and continuation rules.**

For a particular model, the preserved structure and the permitted correspondences must be specified. Agreement on a selected observable is not enough.

A3 establishes sameness **within the specified account**. It does not establish that the account contains every ontologically relevant distinction.

### A3′. Structural sufficiency — absence of bare identity

**[HYP — proposed structuralist commitment]**

> **Complete structural equivalence entails sameness of ontological content. No additional difference consists solely in which otherwise interchangeable labels designate the roles.**

A3′ identifies equivalent **descriptions of the whole**. It does **not** merge distinct roles that a symmetry of the organization exchanges. Two interchangeable roles remain two.

**Completeness condition (mandatory).** Every application that moves from model equivalence (A3) to an ontological conclusion carries:

> `[assuming: the declared organization includes all ontologically relevant structure involved in this identification]`

| Claim | Completeness assumption required? |
|---|---|
| "These descriptions preserve the same declared structure." | No (A3 only) |
| "These descriptions therefore contain no ontological difference." | **Yes**, together with A3′ |

This prevents an equivalence test from silently becoming a completeness proof. Defining a correspondence does not establish that nature contains no further structure.

**Scope of A3′.** A3′ commits the investigation to accounting for identity without bare role identities. It does **not** supply a theory of multiplicity. Whether multiplicity must be grounded in weak discernibility, or in something else, remains open (Q1b).

### A4. Explicit explanatory commitments

**Methodological rule**

> **Every proposed feature must be identified as a definition, an adopted or proposed premise, or a consequence supported by a stated argument.**

The equivalence criterion applies to all declared structure. A result may not be obtained by silently omitting a constraint, orientation, or continuation rule that the argument otherwise uses. The circular pattern to avoid is: omit a distinction, observe that a transformation preserves what remains, conclude that the omitted distinction was unreal.

## 3. Working vocabulary

**Relational role:** a position within a relational organization. The term does not yet specify whether the position has an identity independent of the organization.

**Relational configuration:** a specified organization of distinctions and their connections.

**Relational identity:** what would justify treating a role or structure as the same across different relations or configurations. The criterion remains open (Q3).

**Covariant representation:** a description whose expressions transform under relabeling while the complete relations and their interpretation transform consistently. Label-independent content need not appear only as unchanged numerical quantities.

**Reference role**, in two senses kept distinct:
- *internally distinguished:* the relational organization itself singles out the role;
- *conventionally selected:* a description chooses a role for convenience, and different choices represent the same complete content under the declared equivalence.

A conventional reference need not be physically privileged. Choosing a reference does not remove or consume a constituent.

These terms organize the questions; they do not supply their answers.

## 4. Fundamental questions

| Question | Formulation |
|---|---|
| **Q1a — constitution** `[OPEN]` | What must a relational distinction involve? Are participants prior to relations, constituted through them, or jointly specified? Is there a minimal nontrivial organization, or is demanding a minimum itself an assumption? |
| **Q1b — grounding of multiplicity** `[OPEN]` | What grounds multiplicity within a relational organization? Is an independently specified, non-reflexive relational connection sufficient, necessary, or only one possible basis for distinct roles? |
| **Q2a — arity** `[OPEN]` | What forms and numbers of roles can an irreducible distinction involve? If a non-reflexive connection grounds multiplicity, what determines its irreducible arity? Is a two-role realization minimally admissible, or only minimal once binary form has been assumed? |
| **Q2b — orientation** `[OPEN]` | What distinguishes interchangeable roles from intrinsically ordered or differently functioning roles? When is reversal a relabeling rather than another configuration? *Later application:* when a comparison admits a ratio representation, does inversion (ζ ↦ ζ⁻¹) express relabeling of its roles or a change of configuration? Notation does not settle this (NUMGEN SG-NG-22); RRF OPEN-A is the existing instance. |
| **Q3 — relational identity** `[OPEN]` | What justifies identifying a role across several relations or configurations, especially when several structure-preserving correspondences are possible? |
| **Q4a — admissibility** `[OPEN]` | What determines whether a proposed relational organization is admissible at all? |
| **Q4b — joint compatibility** `[OPEN]` | When can separately admissible organizations coexist, overlap, or connect within one whole? Local admissibility does not by definition guarantee joint compatibility; a compatibility principle must be derived or exposed as an additional premise. |
| **Q5 — continuation** `[OPEN]` | What constitutes persistence, ordering, and transformation within the complete account, and which additional principles, if any, are required? Persistence is not assumed because the same name appears in two descriptions. See OFN SG-OFN-TIME. |
| **Q6 — registration and retention** `[OPEN]` | What additional organization lets one distinction register another and preserve a trace across a continuation? What distinguishes a retained trace from a redescription of the same configuration? |
| **Q7 — internal reference** `[OPEN]` | Under what conditions does relational organization distinguish a reference role, and when is a reference only a convention of description? What remains independent once the declared constraints and representational redundancies are accounted for? |

### 4.1 Notes on Q1b / Q2a — the weak-discernibility route

**[DEF — candidate criterion]** A nontrivial qualitative relation that holds between two roles but cannot hold reflexively *witnesses* their distinctness without assigning either a unique intrinsic label. A symmetric such relation privileges neither role. "Nontrivial" is essential: "is numerically different from" would restate the multiplicity to be explained.

Two separate tasks follow:
- **Entailment:** if such a relation obtains and cannot be reflexive, the roles cannot coincide.
- **Grounding:** explain why this relational fact constitutes or explains the multiplicity, rather than presupposing a domain in which the roles are already distinct.

Settling entailment does not settle grounding.

**Guard on a derived dyad.** Starting from a nonempty *binary* irreflexive relation already selects binary relational form. Showing that a chosen binary relation needs distinct relata is a useful conditional result, not an ontological derivation of the dyad. The dyad is derived only if the preceding commitments justify both the binary form and the minimality criterion. (Compare OFN SG-OFN-PRIMACY.)

### 4.2 Note on Q7 — symmetric roles and selection

**[HEUR — proof sketch]** *If* every candidate role can be carried to every other by a symmetry of the complete structure, *then* no rule based solely on that structure can uniquely designate one of those roles while respecting that symmetry.

*Sketch:* a structure-based rule commutes with the symmetries; its output is therefore fixed by every symmetry; a transitive action on two or more candidates fixes none of them singly.

This becomes `[THM]` once "rule based solely on the structure" is formalized as symmetry-equivariant selection on a declared model. A description may still choose such a role arbitrarily; it may not claim the structure selected it.

A count of the form "*n* roles, one taken as reference, *n − 1* independent" is **not** a foundational consequence. It requires a specified parameterization, constraints, and redundancy. A one-parameter redundancy can produce such a count in an appropriate model; arbitrary relabeling alone does not.

## 5. Central research question

**[OPEN]**

> **Which structures necessarily follow from relational distinction and closure, and which require additional independent commitments?**

A successful step identifies either a justified consequence or a clearly exposed additional premise, not a new name for an unexplained feature.

## 6. Consequence ledger

**[HEUR — provisional dependency assessment]** "Conditional" means conditional on the named premises **and**, wherever the conclusion is about reality rather than about the declared model, on the A3′ completeness condition.

| Candidate structure | Present status | Additional requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **No external intervention** | Follows from A2 (closure) | Whether a given model is actually complete |
| **Sameness of declared structure** | `[DEF]` relative to A3 | A complete specification of what the test preserves |
| **Sameness of ontological content** | Conditional on A3′ | `[assuming: ontological completeness of the declared structure]` |
| **Label-independence of content** | Conditional consequence of A3′; content may be represented covariantly | The completeness condition whenever the conclusion is about reality |
| **No external reference needed to distinguish equivalent descriptions** | Follows from A2 + A3′ (conditional) | Does not exclude an internally distinguished reference role (Q7) |
| **Quotient by representational redundancy** | Motivated by A3′ once equivalence is specified | The actual equivalence relation, its scope, and well-defined dynamics on the quotient |
| **A particular phase-gauge group** | **Not derived.** OFN v0.8 adopts global phase gauge as `[DEF]` scoped to declared phase models (RTG-OFN-DEF-PHASE-GAUGE), separately from relational primacy | Phase structure, group action, complete observables, compatible transition laws |
| **Pole exchange as gauge** | **Not derived** (RRF OPEN-A) | Proof that the exchange preserves the complete relational account |
| **STAR-NOERASE-1 as gauge derivation** | **Not supported.** Scoped injectivity witness only (RECOG record) | It presupposes the quotient it would need to derive |
| **Intrinsic orientation** | Neither required nor excluded | Structure that distinguishes orientation, or a premise admitting it (Q2b) |
| **Multiplicity without bare identities** | `[OPEN]` (Q1b) | An account of what grounds multiplicity |
| **Weak discernibility** | `[OPEN]` candidate grounding strategy | A nontrivial relational witness and an argument for its explanatory role (§4.1) |
| **Dyadic minimality** | `[OPEN]` (Q2a) | Justification of admissible arity and of the minimality criterion |
| **Internal reference role** | `[OPEN]` (Q7) | Distinguish structural selection from conventional choice |
| **An *n − 1* independence count** | Not a foundational consequence | Defined independent parameters, constraints, and redundancy |
| **Ordering or persistence** | Must enter the account explicitly if used (A2) | Its existence, form, and physical interpretation (Q5) |
| **Joint compatibility** | Required for configurations asserted to belong to one whole | A concrete compatibility criterion (Q4b) |
| **Registration or retained memory** | Not a prerequisite of existence (A1) | A mechanism and a criterion of retained distinction (Q6) |

**Strongest defensible first consequence (conditional):**

> **If ontological content is exhausted by complete relational structure (A3′), then differences consisting only in the labels of that structure add no ontological content, and any physical distinction must be represented somewhere in the complete relational account (A2).**

This constrains future models without presupposing a dyad, a ratio, a phase, a preferred orientation, a reference role, or a particular law.

## 7. Discussion record — corrections

`[COR]` **Invariance → covariance.** *Old (Claude, critique round 1):* "Only invariants under relabeling carry content." *New:* physical content is independent of arbitrary labeling; its representations may transform, provided the complete relations and their interpretation transform consistently. *Reason:* the old wording conflated label-independence with numerical invariance and conflicted with the typed-covariance rule of RTG_RRF_REPRESENTATION_REGISTRY v0.7.

`[COR]` **Gauge freedom not derived.** *Old (Claude, critique round 1):* the dyad's phase-gauge freedom "is a consequence of the ontology rather than a modelling choice." *New:* A3′ at most motivates a quotient by representational redundancy; the specific phase-gauge group remains a scoped `[DEF]` in OFN v0.8 (§6 ledger). *Verified against:* OFN v0.8 §1.1.

`[COR]` **Closure and laws.** *Old (Claude, critique round 1):* "dynamics cannot be external … laws must be part of the relational domain." *New:* closure requires an explicit account of any invoked contribution or rule; it does not require laws to be dynamical constituents and does not select a unique account of ordering (A2).

`[COR]` **A3′ and weak discernibility.** *Old (Claude, critique round 2):* "adopting A3′ costs a commitment to weak discernibility." *New:* A3′ concerns equivalence between complete descriptions of a whole; weak discernibility concerns how distinct roles within a structure are distinguished. A3′ makes the absence of a theory of multiplicity explicit; weak discernibility is one candidate answer (Q1b, §4.1). *Reason:* A3 already preserves multiplicity, so A3′ neither explains it nor forces a particular grounding.
