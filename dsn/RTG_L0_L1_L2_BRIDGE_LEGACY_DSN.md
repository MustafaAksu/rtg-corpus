### §12.4 Recursive Feedback and Circular Causality

`[ONTO legacy]` **Emergent feedback hypothesis.** If a new mode $C$ forms, it may become an active participant in the next state rather than a passive diagnostic:

$$
(A,B,C)\rightarrow(A',B',C').
$$

A schematic dynamical form is:

$$
\dot A=f(A,B,C),
\qquad
\dot B=f(B,A,C),
\qquad
\dot C=f(C,A,B).
$$

`[ONTO legacy]` **Circular causality.** The candidate child acts on its parents, schematically:

$$
C = f(A, B), \qquad A' = F(A, C), \qquad B' = F(B, C),
$$

which creates the recursive loop:

$$
(A, B) \;\longrightarrow\; C \;\longrightarrow\; (A, B).
$$

Initialization followed by joint evolution differs from imposing $C=f(A,B)$ at every instant. The latter constrains the triples to a graph; feedback alone proves no state-space enlargement.
