## 5. Health issues

1. **Order sentences without an all-$M$ statement (the CORR lesson).** v0.3.25 adopted "$E'''=o((a+M)^{-1/2})$" and three limit sentences on the strength of grids to $M=10^7$ and an $O$-argument that did not look inside the closed forms. The v0.3.26 round-2 revision then found a second, smaller instance of the same pattern in its own enclosure ($O(\kappa_2\log n/n)$ from a uniform factor, $10^{-5}$ of the term). Proposed rule for RECOG (to be written into CONVENTIONS or the cell template), as sharpened by the co-owner's review: **an order claim must be about the majorant as actually defined and computed, with its domain, quantifiers and remainder terms carried by an explicit analytic proof; a finite grid never substitutes for that proof.** A certified bound $E(a,M,r)\le C(a,r)/(a+M)$ for $M\ge1$ needs an explicit $C$ and the small-$M$ control; the term-by-term table of the drift record §3a is one admissible form, a direct analytic bound or an exact cancellation proof is another — a claim is not demoted to `[NUM]` for lacking the table form. Only a claim with no analytic proof of the stated kind is a grid observation and is tagged `[NUM]`. The v0.3.26 checker already reads a proof-table JSON (C19); the next checkers should require, for any new order sentence, a named certificate gate of whichever form the proof takes.
2. **Late-slip tests dominated by the reference tolerance.** The 73-slip containment tests of the start-offset and drift cells compare $|\varepsilon_k|$ with $E_k+L_{\rm tol}$. The criterion is the ratio $L_{\rm tol}/E_k$, not a universal $M$ threshold: where it exceeds $1$ (in the sampled orbits, the latest slips, $M\approx2\times10^5$) the comparison is a consistency check, not a sharp test of $E_k$ — recorded with the caveat both times. This limits what the experiment can confirm; it says nothing against the analytic bound. A sharper reference $L$ (longer orbits, or an independent estimate) is needed before an envelope is "tested" where the ratio is large.
3. **Notices.** None filed in the receivers' inboxes until 29 Oct, while two of them decide whether the `[assuming:]` qualifiers of the known-$a$ chain can be dropped; RECOG's own inbox unread at two checkpoints. Both corrected on 29 Oct (§7). The v0.3.25 post-release PR #90 is review-accepted and unmerged since 27 Oct (its notice texts now marked superseded for dispatch).
4. **Dependence on one Dyadic ceiling.** $K_\epsilon$ (98 %), $\gamma_2$ ($2C_7$ of $212.6$) and the B1-L2 rows all hang on $C_7$. Any Dyadic sharpening propagates; any Dyadic correction would too.
5. **The in-block residual.** $c_P(r)Q^\dagger$ at $r\ge3$ is the largest term of the log-free envelope in the sampled budget ($14.4$ of $19.15$ at $a=625$, $r=5$, illustrative $b$-width $2$; the widest-range all-$M$ constant $58.0$ is dominated by the start-offset term instead) and was flagged as the next lever already in v0.3.25's records. It is the first thing to sharpen *if* a consumer needs a narrower known-$a$ window than the $1.02$–$1.87$ gains of v0.3.26 give; by itself it does not decide the research priority (§6).
6. **GitHub hygiene.** Six stacked cell PRs whose heads are in `main` remain open; PR #90 unmerged; PR #103 (OFN advisory) and #104 (v0.3.26 post-release) open. Harmless, but the PR list no longer reflects the work queue.

A short working recipe in `records/recog/` would let another stream reuse it and make the next release cheaper to review; no new method is being invented.
