Structural endpoint (`[ALG]`/`[RESULT]`, scoped to RECOG's registered single-dyad carriers at the certified order): **the package contains no mechanism by which a token writes into an independent retained observable.** Carriers with independent retention update smoothly and have no registered event-gated channel; carriers that respond to tokens are counters (log-equivalent). This is not a no-memory theorem for enlarged states, and it is not a statement about physics.

## 1. To the Ontology note owner

**Payload.** The single-dyad realisation separates the two properties an event might be expected to combine:

- **independent retention without a token** (R9: comparator data do not determine the retained phase; nothing happens at a distinguished instant);
- **dynamically distinguished tokens without independent retention** (the fold loses local inverse uniqueness, `E`, with nothing registered retaining the branch; the 7-gap's retention is bookkeeping, $R_{\log}$).

**Questions routed upstream (interpretation/design — RECOG does not answer them):**

1. Does the ontology *require* that a recognition event couple a token to independent retention (token → $R_{\rm ind}$)? If so, the current single-dyad substrate does not realise recognition events, and the ontology must say where the missing coupling lives.
2. Does the ontology *permit* an event whose consequence is `E` (loss of inverse uniqueness) without retained branch data? The fold is RECOG's cleanest structural transition, and it is of this kind.
3. Does the no-erasure `[HYP]` constrain the answer? Note that RECOG's `E` is registry-relative: an unregistered history carrier could retain the branch; RECOG forbids reading the fold as "erasure" in any ontological sense.
4. Is $R_{\log}$-type retention (an event count plus a clock) ever sufficient for recognition, or is it excluded as tautological?

RECOG supplies only the substrate classification above; any reading belongs to the ontology note.

## 2. To the TDR-G owner

**Guard first.** RECOG does **not** indicate that recognition memory "must live in the network". Carrier enlargement (multi-dyad / network / history structure) is one of two successor routes RECOG lists, and it is recorded as a routing `[HYP]` only. The single-dyad $z$-chart does not transport linear network closure ($h(z_{AB})+h(z_{BC})+h(z_{CA})=0$, not $\sum z_e=0$); SG-R5 and the RECOG network guard remain in force. No RECOG equation is offered for network use.

**Target posed (if TDR-G chooses to take it up):**

> **Does a discrete/network token leave a non-token-equivalent retained network observable?**

That is, look for the network analogue of RECOG's C2: a token-gated change in an independently defined carrier — e.g. an incidence, holonomy or history object — that is **not** reducible to the event count, the token log, or the clock. RECOG's criteria may help frame the test:

- the carrier must be defined independently of the token predicate (otherwise it is a log);
- the token-gated contribution must be identifiable — either through an explicitly registered event channel or through a non-smooth signature that a smooth background law cannot absorb;
- conditioning must be on the appropriate comparator/quotient data, not on the full state (full-state conditioning is Markov-trivial for autonomous dynamics).

A positive answer would supply what RECOG's single-dyad substrate lacks; a negative answer would further constrain what a recognition event can mean. Either way the result belongs to TDR-G.
