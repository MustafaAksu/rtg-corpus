**Who and why.** COIL (satellite study, not RTG; PI authorisation of this request: 14 Nov 2026). Three COIL lines closed without a discriminating result; its Charter §9.4 now asks, before any new run, for a quantitative statement that survives the "strong null" (generic bifurcation behaviour) — or an account of why its model family cannot give one. COIL needs the literature to state that null precisely.
**The object.** MM-1's reduced dyad is the explicit-Euler damped rotor map φ⁺ = φ + w, w⁺ = (1 − a) w + k F(φ), F periodic and odd (sine or triangle); equivalently φ_{n+2} − (2 − a) φ_{n+1} + (1 − a) φ_n = k F(φ_n). COIL's own statements: a complex unit-circle crossing at k s = a with cos ω = 1 − a/2 (`[THM]`, s = −F′(0)); a chaos onset at κ = k s / a ≈ 2.65–2.80 while a rises 5.2× (`[NUM]`, three-node realisation, locked runs); no crossing in the Euler–Cromer ordering.
**Request — literature support on this topic (not a verdict on one work):**
1. Is this map class studied under another name (forward-Euler pendulum, delayed-force or "non-symplectic" standard map, sampled-data loop), and what is published on its route beyond the first crossing — in particular anything that explains, predicts or contradicts a chaos onset at a near-constant multiple of the crossing threshold as a varies?
2. What does generic theory fix **quantitatively** after a Neimark–Sacker crossing on a compact phase space (resonance tongues, torus breakdown, first positive exponent), and what does it leave model-specific? COIL needs this to write its null.
3. Independent **measured** data for systems whose linear block is this recurrence with a physically real one-step lag — e.g. discrete-generation populations with lagged density dependence (the delayed logistic map x_{n+1} = r x_n (1 − x_{n−1}) has the same linear block at a = 1): onset and what follows.
